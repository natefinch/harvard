# The Manuscript's Claims and Rumors

Source: `../Harvard, Massachusetts.md`, a deliberately tongue-in-cheek "100% true" exposé. Each claim below is rated as **True**, **Partly true**, **Folklore** (a local story, not confirmed), or **Satire/False**.

## Big claims
| Claim | Rating | Reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named after, but unrelated to, Harvard University | True | Probably named by Josiah Willard in honor of Harvard College |
| About 5,000 people | Outdated | 6,851 in 2020 |
| Settled in 1658 by the "Dillard family" | Partly true | Settlement began in 1658; the Willards were a prominent early family. "Dillard" is the disguise |
| Cyclists vanish; avoid Cleaves Hill and Lovers Lane | Folklore/satire | Both roads exist. The hilly roads really are popular with cyclists |
| Local "Narragansett" tribe | False | The local people were the Nashaway (Nipmuc) |
| "Chapiokhen" means "hades / place of the dead" | Invented | Do not present as a real translation |
| Shabikin was the old name for the area past the Nashua | True | One of the five 1734 districts, later taken for Devens |
| Strange disappearances and twisted crops in Shabikin | Folklore | No source found |
| Fort Devens taken in 1917 | True | Camp Devens was built in 1917 for WWI training |
| Hundreds of "recruit" deaths in the first year | Partly true | The 1918 influenza pandemic devastated the camp |
| Roaring and lights explained as "scientific experimentation" | Folklore | Artillery and training explain most of it |
| Millions spent on WWII buildings "for purposes unknown" | Partly true | Massive WWII expansion did happen, for ordinary purposes |
| Army Security and Intelligence training in the 1950s–70s | True | The Army Security Agency school was at Devens |
| Superfund site in 1989, then closed | True | It closed in 1996 and became the Devens enterprise zone |
| Booms still shake houses | Partly true | The Army reserve training area is still in use |
| Mirror Lake was once "Hell Pond" and renamed by the military | Folklore | Unverified. It is a deep kettle pond; the cave connection is legend |
| "Commonwealth Nuclear Systems" with Soviet tech and Russian protests | Satire | The real company is Commonwealth Fusion Systems (SPARC tokamak) in Devens |
| Four officers, a detective, and a search-and-rescue lab | Invented cast | Use as is; the characters are fictional |
| New library in the old Bromfield School | True | The janitor-screams story is folklore |

## The rumor list (all Folklore unless noted)
1. **The moving corpse**: kids lined up with flashlights to look into a crypt at the cemetery by the school (Center Cemetery).
2. **The phantom buggy**: a man drives a buggy down Still River Road on Halloween.
3. **The crypt telephone**: in a Still River cemetery crypt, the last occupant had a phone line installed so she could call from the other side. It has since been removed. *This is the central hook of the game.*
4. **Buried in his bed**: a headstone on Poor Farm Road for an epidemic victim nobody would touch.
5. **The broken-heart stone**: a headstone left broken because the surviving spouse died of a broken heart.
6. **The servant's stone**: a lone headstone behind the Congregational church, for a servant buried there against the rules of the time.
7. **Wagons in the pond**: wagons left behind when Bare Hill Pond was flooded, and carts that fell through the ice.
8. **Library basement screams**: a janitor accidentally locked in for a summer.
9. **Lovers Lane**: the manuscript's heading says lovers died there by suicide. Handle with care if used.
10. **Bloody Hollow**: a heading only, with no story. See open-leads.md.

## How the game used this material
- **Used directly**: the crypt telephone (as the main case), the phantom buggy, the moving corpse, buried in his bed, the broken-heart stone, library screams, and drowned wagons and road.
- **Reframed**: "Fruitlands cannibals" and "the orchard witch" became jokes attached to fictional characters (a beet-soup commune and a skeptical apple witch).
- **Rejected as fact**: the invented Indigenous etymologies and the curse explanation. In the game, the legends are *symptoms* of a modern resonator, not an ancient curse.
