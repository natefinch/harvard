# Town History Timeline

Labels: [Sourced] checked against a source · [General] background, not re-checked · [OSM] map data.

## Before colonization
- **c. 5500 BC onward**: The Nashua River floodplain, now Oxbow National Wildlife Refuge, shows evidence of long Indigenous habitation. USFWS presumes this was Nipmuc. [Sourced: USFWS Oxbow]
- The local people were the **Nashaway** (a Nipmuc-affiliated band) centered on the Nashua valley near Lancaster. The manuscript says "Narragansett," which is wrong; the Narragansett lived in Rhode Island. [General]
- Nipmuc arrowheads have been found on the Fruitlands property. The source gives the hill's Native name as "Makamacheckamucks." [Sourced: Trustees/Fruitlands, as reported; spelling unverified]

## Colonial era
- **1658**: Settlement begins along the Lancaster–Groton road, on land bought from the Nashaway sachem **Sholan** and his nephew **George Tahanto**. Their names survive in Harvard road names ("Sholan Circle," "Tahanto Trail") and in Fiske Warren's "Tahanto" enclave. [Sourced: Wikipedia; road names from OSM]
- **1732**: Harvard is incorporated from parts of Lancaster, Groton, and Stow. The charter required a minister within two years. The name probably came from **Josiah Willard**, honoring Harvard College; the town is otherwise unrelated to the university. [Sourced]
  - The manuscript's "Dillard family, pillars of the community since 1658" is a lightly disguised version of the Willard family. Willard Lane and Willard Street appear on the map.
- **1733–1757**: The first minister was Rev. John Seccombe. [Sourced]
- **1734**: The town was organized into five districts: **Oak Hill, Bare Hill, Still River, Old Mill,** and **Shabikin**. Shabikin is the northwest corner later taken for Fort Devens. [Sourced]
- **1769**: Followers of the dissenting preacher **Shadrack Ireland** build the **Square House**. [Sourced]
- **1781**: **Mother Ann Lee** arrives. The Square House becomes the core of the first Shaker community in Massachusetts and the second in the US. [Sourced] Ann Lees Road is on the map. [OSM]

## 19th century
- **1794**: The Shaker office building is built. In the 1920s it was moved to Fruitlands. [Sourced]
- **1830–1860**: The **Benjamin Ball Pencil Co.** operates in the Old Mill district. [Sourced]
- **June 1843 – January 1844**: **Fruitlands**, the transcendentalist utopia led by **Bronson Alcott** and **Charles Lane**, runs on Prospect Hill. [Sourced]
  - Members followed a strict vegan diet: no animal products and no animal labor, so humans pulled the plows. They drank only water and wore linen rather than cotton (slave-grown) or wool (taken from sheep). Lamplight was limited because whale oil and tallow were forbidden. [General]
  - Louisa May Alcott, then 10, lived there and later satirized it in *Transcendental Wild Oats*. Thoreau and Emerson visited. The experiment collapsed in its first winter. [Sourced/General]
- **1850s**: The Harvard Shakers peak at about 200 members. [Sourced]
- The Shaker hymn **"The Humble Heart"** (Eunice Wyeth and Thomas Hammond) came from the Harvard community. [Sourced]
- Throughout this period, apples and cider were major products. Orchards still define the town's identity. [Sourced]
- **c. 1890**: Fewer than 40 Shakers remain. [Sourced]

## 20th century
- **1914**: **Clara Endicott Sears** opens Fruitlands as a museum. Her estate was called **The Pergolas**. [Sourced]
- **1917**: **Camp Devens** is built on land taken from Harvard (including Shabikin), Lancaster, Ayer, and Shirley to train WWI draftees. [Sourced: USFWS]
  - **1918**: The influenza pandemic hit Camp Devens catastrophically, with thousands sick and hundreds dead within weeks in September and October. This is the real explanation behind the manuscript's "hundreds of deaths in its first year." [General]
- **1917–1918**: The Shaker village closes and is sold to **Fiske Warren**. [Sourced]
- **1918–1938**: Warren founds and runs the **Tahanto single-tax enclave**, a Georgist land-value-tax colony. It ends with his death in 1938. [Sourced]
- **1922**: The Shaker office building, moved to Fruitlands, opens as the first Shaker museum. [Sourced]
- **1931**: Fort Devens becomes a permanent installation. [General]
- **1933**: **Oak Ridge Observatory** (the George R. Agassiz Station) opens on Pinnacle Road. [Sourced]
- **1939**: 248 of Sears' 458 acres are taken for Fort Devens expansion. Much of that land is now in the Oxbow refuge. [Sourced]
- **WWII**: The base grows enormously. **1950s–70s**: It is used by Army Security Agency and intelligence schools. The manuscript's "purposes unknown" is mostly ordinary Army expansion. [General]
- **1953**: Fr. Leonard Feeney is excommunicated. His group, the Slaves of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, co-founded with Catherine Goddard Clarke, moves to **Still River in 1958**. It later splits; one branch reconciles with Rome and becomes the core of today's **St. Benedict Abbey**, while the sisters continue nearby. [Sourced/General] *Never use these real names in fiction.*
- **1974**: **Oxbow National Wildlife Refuge** is established. [Sourced]
- **1989**: The Harvard Shaker Village Historic District is listed on the National Register. [Sourced] Fort Devens is listed as an EPA Superfund site. [General; also stated by the manuscript, and true]
- **1996**: Fort Devens closes as an active base. It is redeveloped as the Devens Regional Enterprise Zone, with a smaller Army reserve training area remaining. [General]

## 21st century
- **2001**: About 120 acres in Harvard are added to Oxbow NWR. [Sourced]
- **2005**: Oak Ridge Observatory closes. [Sourced]
- **2020**: The population is **6,851** across about 27 sq mi. The manuscript's "about 5,000" is outdated. [Sourced]
- **2021**: St. Benedict Abbey elects a new abbot. Real name withheld here; do not use it. [Sourced]
- **2020s**: **Commonwealth Fusion Systems** builds its SPARC tokamak demonstration fusion device in Devens. [General; OSM point used for location]
  - The manuscript's "Commonwealth Nuclear Systems," Soviet technology, and Russian objections are satire. The tokamak design did originate in the USSR, which is the grain of truth. SPARC is not "Super Powered ARC."
- Today **Fruitlands** is run by **The Trustees**: 210 acres at 102 Prospect Hill Rd. It has the farmhouse, Shaker Museum, Wayside Visitor Center, galleries, a café, a fairy-house forest, and a craft fair, plus a Halloween-season morris-dance tradition. It looks out on **Mt. Wachusett** and **Mt. Monadnock**. [Sourced]
