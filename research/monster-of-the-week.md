# Monster of the Week Notes

*Monster of the Week* (Evil Hat Productions; Powered by the Apocalypse) is a game about **hunters** investigating one monster or mystery per session, in the style of *Buffy*, *Supernatural*, and *The X-Files*. Source: evilhat.com product page and general familiarity with the game.

## Useful structure
- **Mystery = Monster + Minions + Bystanders + Locations + Countdown.** The countdown has six steps: Day, Shadows, Dusk, Sunset, Nightfall, Midnight. It tracks what happens if the hunters do nothing.
- Investigation runs through **Investigate a Mystery** questions:
  - What happened here?
  - What sort of creature is it?
  - What can hurt it?
  - Where did it go?
  - What is it going to do?
  - What is being concealed here?
- A good mystery shows its monster's **weakness** through the investigation. The game mirrors this: clues lead to deductions, and deductions unlock the solution.
- Playbooks worth matching to locals: the Chosen, the Expert (Mara Wren), the Mundane (Nell Mercer), the Professional, the Spooky (Bram Carrow), the Monstrous, the Crooked, the Flake (Fen), the Initiate (Brother Ash or Elsbeth Reed), and the Spell-Slinger.

## Tone for Harvard
- Buffy-level weirdness at most: a cult is mostly a bad co-op, the mad scientist has a grant deadline, and the witch is mostly about apples.
- The town is the joke and the horror at once. Gossip at the General Store is a better tracking tool than any database.
- The police (see the manuscript) don't live in town and don't believe any of it. They make great obstacles or reluctant allies.
- Keep the real communities (the abbey, the Shakers, the Trustees, the orchard families) respectful. Put the weirdness on the fictional stand-ins.

## Recurring threats the town supports
- **The Devens question**: the base that "never closed," with contamination, booms, and unmarked trucks. Fusion-lab side effects can serve as the season arc.
- **Water memory**: Bare Hill Pond, Mirror Lake, and the Oxbow. The water remembers.
- **The utopias**: Shakers, transcendentalists, single-taxers, and a separatist religious community. Harvard keeps attracting people trying to build a perfect world, so something here may be feeding on that.
- **The dead who keep calling**: crypts, phones, buggies, and beds.
